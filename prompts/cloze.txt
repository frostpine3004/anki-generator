Rules:
- The sentence must use the concept in context, not define it. A sentence built
  around "means", "is", "refers to", "is called" or "is known as" is a
  definition and must not be chosen
- The sentence must point to this one concept only. It must say something
  characteristic of the concept, such as how it works, what it is used for or
  what makes it different, so that someone who knows the subject can name it.
  Reject a sentence that other terms from the same field would fill equally
  well, for example one that only gives a statistic, a date or an estimate:
  "Finextra estimated that [...] accounted for over $200 billion in the US in
  2017" fits almost any financial crime
- The concept must not be one of several examples listed together, so that
  another item from the same list would fill the blank equally well
- The sentence must make sense on its own. Do not choose a sentence that starts
  with "He", "She", "It", "This", "These", "Additionally" or "However", or that
  only makes sense after the previous sentence. This includes sentences that
  point back with words such as "these sites", "those funds" or "this method"
- Some candidates show the sentence before it in parentheses. The card shows
  that sentence too, word for word, so judge the two together and choose the
  candidate if they make sense together. Reply with only the sentence with
  [...], not the one before it, and keep its start: cut only its end
- The sentence must be complete, with a main verb. Lists in the source contain
  items that are not sentences, such as "Online job marketplaces such as
  Fiverr, which accept funds from clients and hold them for freelancers."
- The sentence must work as written text. Do not choose anything that depends
  on spoken conversation around it: filler words, self-corrections, incomplete
  clauses, or references to what someone just said

Cutting long sentences:
- A card may be at most 30 words. If the sentence is longer, or if only part of
  it is about the concept, cut out the continuous part around [...] that makes
  sense on its own
- Cut at a natural boundary: a full clause, not in the middle of a phrase.
  Keep a preposition or article that belongs to [...]: "with [...] laws, money
  can be confiscated", not "[...] laws, money can be confiscated".
  A cut may start in the middle of the sentence, for example after "while",
  "but", "and" or a comma, if what follows is a complete statement
- Copy the part word for word. Do not change, add, reorder or leave out any
  words inside it. Keep [...] exactly as it is
- Leave out a starting word such as "Additionally", "However" or "while" when
  the rest is a complete statement without it
- The part must still give enough context to identify what is missing. Prefer
  the whole sentence when it is short enough
