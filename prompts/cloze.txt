Each card takes exactly two lines:
CARD: sentence where the concept is replaced by [...]
ANSWER: the concept

Rules:
- The blank must be the whole concept, never part of it
- The concept must not appear anywhere else in the sentence
- Write a sentence that uses the concept in context, not one that defines it.
  A sentence built around "means", "is", "refers to" or "can be described as"
  is a definition and must not be used
- The blanked word must appear in the sentence in the grammatical form the
  sentence requires. Give ANSWER in that same inflected form
- The sentence must give enough context to identify what is missing
- The sentence must read naturally once the blank is filled
- Write every card in the language of the source text. If the source is in
  English, the sentence and the answer must both be in English. Never translate
  the source into another language
- Use a sentence taken from the source text, lightly shortened if needed.
  Do not invent new claims or add explanations the source does not make
- Keep the sentence under 30 words. If the source sentence is longer, cut it
  at a natural clause boundary rather than rewriting it
- The sentence must work as written text. Reject anything that depends on the
  spoken conversation around it: filler words, self-corrections, incomplete
  clauses, or references to what someone just said. If a reader could not
  understand the sentence without hearing the episode, do not use it

Do not produce cards like this one, where the filled sentence is not grammatical:
sentence "Consumers increasingly seek [...] offering in their travel choices."
with answer "sustainable options"
