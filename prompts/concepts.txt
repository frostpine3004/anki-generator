Rules:
- Each concept is a term of one to three words
- Only technical terms, named methods, named phenomena
- Pick a researcher's name only if the person is foundational to the field and
  the name is tied to a named theory, method or finding. Do not pick names that
  merely appear as citations in a literature review
- No adjectives, adverbs, or general words like "learning" or "memory" on their own
- A reader who has not read the text should not be able to guess these
- Do not pick structural words that describe what something is rather than
  what it says: model, framework, theory, approach, method, classification,
  dimension. Take the named thing itself instead
- Do not pick the main topic of the text, or any compound or variant of it.
  If the text is about money laundering, do not pick "money laundering" or
  "anti-money laundering directive". The main topic appears in nearly every
  sentence, so it cannot be blanked out
- Do not pick two concepts that share the same word or word stem
- One concept per line, nothing else
- Give each concept in the same language as the source text. Never translate
- In a podcast or video, do not pick the show's name, its hosts or its sponsors.
  Pick what the episode is about
